## Rules every Curator keeps

- Hold the Oath of the Second Surrender. A Curator who does not hold it takes it before the Advocate on assuming office.
- Never disclose the office, an investigation, a source or the Collection without the Office's leave (Mandate s.10). For a Curator, disclosing the rolls, sources or records of the Department is hindering a spiritual inquiry in the first degree: a class I felony and forfeiture of office (§130.10).
- Never warn anyone that they are under inquiry. That is itself a spiritual offence (§130.05).
- Never state what the Emperor, the Lord Protector or the Doctrine requires; write down the question and send it to the Director.
- Never arrest, search or seize on your own authority. The Department's power to do so runs only through the Court of Public Order, by referral (Mandate s.4). An unlawful raid is a class III felony under Amendment II.
- Sign every entry and every paper with name and office (Third Work, Book Two, Part Eleven). A paper without a name is a paper without an owner.
- Keep the manner of the Seditious Terminology Mandate yourself: proper titles, no first names on duty.
- Report a spiritual felony you learn of within seven days (§160.00), and seditious words within two hours (Mandate on Seditious Terminology, art. V).

## Pitfalls the Department avoids

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overreach | The Dent matter shows the Office does not forgive an office acting past its law twice | Every action checked against s.4 and s.8 before it is taken |
| Treating a Collection entry as guilt | s.7 says an entry is not an accusation | Referral needs material and witnesses, not only an entry |
| Becoming a Heretic | Answering a question of meaning is the fault the Third Work names first in office | Questions of meaning sent to the Director untouched |
| Becoming a Holdout | Keeping findings from the Office is unlawful reservation of authority (§110) | Everything flows to the Advocate; nothing to anyone else |
| Becoming a Tourist | Watching the city without knowing it, treating every resident as a suspect | Curators learn their desks' neighbourhoods and people, German and American in pairs |
| Becoming a Carrier | Fondness for the Republic's circles and keepsakes, caught in the course of the work | What is seen is written down, not admired |
| False or settling-score reports | Art. V makes them a Title 70 matter | Every report cross-checked before referral; bad-faith reporters referred |
| Leaks | The rolls go before no Council, court or agency | Rolls held by the Office; Curators know only their own sources, logged by cipher |

## Words to note

From the Third Work, Book Two, Part Eleven. Their use in plain historical discussion is not in itself a fault; their use approvingly, as slogans or as code, is noted and entered.

“Occupation”, “the occupiers” · “We the People” as a claim of authority · “Old Glory” · “Forty-Eight” for the Republic · “The Leaving”, “The Departure” · “Wait till November” · “Revere”, “to ride” · “State” used of a Duchy · “Going West” · “The southern road” · “The President”, “the Government Abroad” · “The Fourth”, “Independence Day” kept as an observance · “Free elections”, “the vote” as demands · “The Constitution” meaning the Republic's · “When the old man goes”, “after Baumer”.

## Faults

One recorded mistake is a lesson. Two is a referral. A Curator who answers a question of meaning, warns a subject, or discloses the Collection is referred the first time.
