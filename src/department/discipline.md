## Rules every Curator keeps

- Never disclose the office, an investigation, a source or the Collection without the Office's leave (s.10).
- Never warn anyone that they are under inquiry. That is itself a spiritual offence (s.7).
- Never state what the Emperor, the Lord Protector or the Doctrine requires; write down the question and send it up.
- Never arrest, search or seize on the Department's own authority.
- Sign every entry and every paper with name and office (Third Work, Book Two, Part 11).
- Keep the manner of the Seditious Terminology Mandate yourself: proper titles, no first names on duty.

## Pitfalls the Department avoids

| Pitfall | Why it matters | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overreach | The Dent matter shows the Office does not forgive an office acting past its law twice | Every action checked against s.4 and s.8 before it is taken |
| Treating a Collection entry as guilt | s.7 says an entry is not an accusation | Referral needs material and witnesses, not only an entry |
| Becoming a Heretic | Answering a question of meaning is the fault the Third Work names first in office | Questions of meaning marked and sent up untouched |
| Becoming a Holdout | Keeping findings from the Office | Everything flows to the Advocate; nothing to anyone else |
| False or settling-score reports | Art. V makes them a Title 70 matter | Every report cross-checked before referral; bad-faith reporters referred |
| Leaks | The rolls go before no Council, court or agency | Rolls held by the Office; Curators know only their own sources |

## Faults

One recorded mistake is a lesson. Two is a referral. A Curator who answers a question of meaning, warns a subject, or discloses the Collection is referred the first time.
