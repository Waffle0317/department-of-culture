Each step builds on the one before: the documents from Step 1 are the tools used in Steps 2 and 3.

## Step 1: Organize and write the documents

**Goal:** a Department with sworn staff, clear rules and the paperwork to run on, before any investigation starts.

### Organize

- Take the Oath of the Second Surrender and enroll on the Party registry.
- Give up any other office of the Capital, as the mandate requires.
- Recruit Curators for the Public Advocate to appoint: sworn members first, German–American pairs where possible.
- Split the work into four desks: Print and Broadcast · Stage, Music and Societies · Schools and Instruction · Public Bodies and Observances.

### Write the documents

| Document | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Curator Handbook | The rules every Curator follows: what they may do, what they may not, how to stay confidential |
| The Collection | The secret register of people, works and observations, with one fixed entry format |
| Report Form | How public servants file the two-hour sedition reports |
| Prior Review Order | Tells licensing and funding offices to submit cultural works before release |
| Referral Form | The package sent to the Advocate: facts, evidence, witnesses, the law broken |
| Weekly Report Template | What the Advocate receives from the Department every week |

**Done when:** Curators are sworn and appointed, and every document above is approved by the Advocate.

## Step 2: Watch and gather

**Goal:** put the whole cultural life of the Capital under watch, using the documents from Step 1.

- **Map the city.** List every theatre, club, school, newspaper, broadcaster, hall and congregation, and which office licenses or funds it.
- **Send out the Prior Review Orders.** Media, Records, the Comptroller and the schools submit works before release. The Department permits, alters or withholds each within a day.
- **Open the report lines.** Two-hour sedition reports from public servants, Box 70 tips, Workers' Advocacy Wardens, the Germanic Desk.
- **Put Curators in the field.** They attend performances, meetings, classes and Party observances, undeclared where useful.
- **Log everything in the Collection.** Every report and observation, the same hour, signed by the Curator.

**The Department never** arrests, searches or seizes, and never decides what the Doctrine means. Those go up in Step 3.

**Done when:** every cultural venue in the city is on the map, and reports are arriving and being logged daily.

## Step 3: Refer and report

**Goal:** every finding reaches the Public Advocate complete and lawful, and he always knows what the Department is doing.

**Each matter ends one of three ways:**

1. **Keep and watch.** Nothing proven yet; it stays on the Collection.
2. **Refer to the Advocate.** A spiritual offence, or a question of what the Doctrine means, goes up on the Referral Form.
3. **Refer to the Office.** If an arrest, search or seizure is needed, the Office has the DA compel NYPD, ABI or NSB, or tasks the VSK.

**Report every week.** Works reviewed, withheld or altered; new Collection entries; referrals and their outcome; questions waiting on the Advocate.

**Improve as you go.** Propose new banned terms from what Curators hear, retrain any Curator who slips, and fix the documents when cases show a gap.

**Done when:** the Advocate gets a report every week and never has to send a referral back for missing material.
