- **Escalate slowly.** Start with cautions and observation; save the Title 70 register and referrals for repeat or public conduct. A player fined on their first line of dialogue just logs off.
- **Leave a trail players can find.** A Curator sitting in on a club meeting, a rumoured informant, a play suddenly withdrawn: these give the resistance and the clubs something to react to.
- **Give penitents a real path.** The Second Surrender and Recognition are in the lore for a reason; let players redeem a character in a scene, not only lose one.
- **Keep paperwork short and visible.** Every action leaves a dated, signed record, so staff can review it and nobody argues afterwards about what happened.
- **Stay out of other factions' jobs.** No arrests, raids or seizures by the Department means the NYPD, ABI and VSK keep their scenes too.
- **Separate IC from OOC.** Collection entries are in-character; nothing a player says OOC goes in them.
- **Check with staff** before any storyline that removes a character from play for long, such as a lengthy detention.

## Scenes the Department is good for

| Scene | What it gives other players |
| --- | --- |
| A Curator at the back of a club meeting | The society has to decide what to say in front of them |
| A play permitted with alterations | The theatre argues the cuts, performs around them, or defies them |
| A rumoured informant | Suspicion inside a group; a hunt for the source |
| A Title 70 caution delivered in person | A character choice: back down, or escalate |
| A question of meaning sent up | A visible wait on the Advocate's ruling; both sides lobby |
