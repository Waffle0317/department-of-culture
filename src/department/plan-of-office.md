## Opening statement

I will run the Department of Culture exactly as the Mandate on Culture (10 October 1962) builds it: an investigative body that watches, gathers, records and refers, and nothing more. Its strength is that every finding reaches the Public Advocate clean, confidential and inside the law, so that no office can say the Advocacy overreached.

The Office has already shown, in the Notice and Determination regarding Richard Dent, what happens when an office acts past what the law gives it. My Department will never hand anyone that argument. My plan has three aims:

1. **Stand the Department up in 48 hours.** Curators sworn, the Collection opened, the intake for reports running.
2. **Bring the Capital's cultural life under standing review within the first week.** Prior-review orders issued, every channel of report connected.
3. **Make the Department the Advocate's most reliable instrument within the first month.** A weekly report on his desk, every referral complete, no question of meaning ever answered by a Curator.

## First week and first month

The first week puts the whole cultural life of the Capital under standing review; the first month turns that into a routine the Advocate can rely on.

### First week: coverage

1. **Map the field.** Build a register of every theatre, cinema, club, society, school, newspaper, broadcaster, hall and congregation in the Capital, and which office licenses or funds each.
2. **Extend standing orders under s.6** to every office on that map: the Comptroller (business licences), the schools, the Germanic Desk for the German-language press and societies it supports.
3. **Connect the Workers' Advocacy.** Wardens already report on the mood and loyalty of places of work; agree with the President that anything touching cultural matter or seditious words is passed to the Department.
4. **Brief every office on the two-hour duty.** A short notice from the Office reminding public servants what to report, where, and that a good-faith report carries no penalty.
5. **Attend the first gatherings.** Curators begin attending public performances, meetings and observances, undeclared where useful (s.7).
6. **Clear the Dent records.** Finish the examination and hand the Advocate a written summary; surrendered copies go to the Office for Information, Records, and Patents.

### First month: rhythm

- **Weekly report to the Advocate:** matters reviewed, permitted, withheld or altered; new Collection entries; referrals made and their status; reports received and their source type; questions of meaning awaiting him.
- **Calendar of the Days.** Plan observation of Watch Night (18 April), the Day of the Honours, Deutscher Tag and every Party observance: who attends, what is checked.
- **Terminology list kept current.** The Department proposes new banned terms from what it hears; the Advocate decides, since what counts as seditious is a question of meaning.
- **Schools and instruction.** Review curricula and textbooks still carrying Republic names ("State" for Duchy and the like) under the Public Names Order, and refer corrections to the Office.
- **Train the Curators** in the mandate, the Spiritual Penal Code and evidence handling. One recorded mistake is a lesson; two is a referral.

## Proposed technical corrections

The Mandate on Culture has three cross-references that point to the wrong section; I would offer the Advocate corrected text for his approval rather than act on my own reading.

| Where | Says | Should say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| s.3, definition of "Curator" | appointed under section ten | section nine |
| s.4 | referral as provided in section nine | section eight |
| s.7 | refusing a production "required under this section", but s.7 requires none | the production power in s.6, or a production power to be added |

Two further points for the Advocate's direction:

- **Curators and Court process.** s.4 forbids the Department to arrest or seize, while the Court of Public Order Mandate has its process executed by Curators. I read them together: Curators act on the Court's process, never on their own. A one-line clarification would settle it.
- **Missing instruments.** The Title 70 Act and the Spiritual Penal Code are cited throughout but were not in my reading set. I would read both before taking office.
