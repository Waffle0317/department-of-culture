> Promulgated by the Office of the Public Advocate of New York under Section 6 of the New York Home Rule Act and Articles I, IV and VII of the Constitution of the Imperial American Protectorate.
>
> 10th October, New York, 1962. The Public Advocate of New York, **Landon LeBlanc**.

## Part One: General Provisions

### General Provisions

**§ 1.00 Short title.** This document shall be known as the Spiritual Penal Law.

**§ 1.05 General purposes.** The general purposes of this document are:

1. To proscribe conduct which unjustifiably threatens the public morality of the Capital or the spiritual unity of the organs of the State with the People;
2. To give fair warning of the nature of the conduct proscribed and of the sentences authorized upon conviction;
3. To define the act or omission, and the accompanying mental state, which constitute each offense;
4. To differentiate on reasonable grounds between serious and minor offenses, and to prescribe proportionate penalties therefor; and
5. To provide for the correction and instruction of persons convicted, and for their restoration to the body of the People.

**§ 1.10 Authority.** This document is promulgated pursuant to section six of the New York Home Rule Act, which charges the Public Advocate with the public morality and spiritual unity of the organs of the State with the People and authorizes him to investigate and adjudicate such matters; to Article IV of the Constitution of the Imperial American Protectorate; and to subdivisions C, D and F of Amendment I thereto. The Office of the Public Advocate may also prosecute using the Criminal Penal Code under specific circumstances.

### Article 5: Construction

**§ 5.00 Spiritual Penal Law not strictly construed.** The general rule that a penal statute is to be strictly construed does not apply to this document. Its provisions shall be construed according to the fair import of their terms to promote justice and effect the objects of the law.

**§ 5.05 Questions of meaning.** Every question of meaning arising under this document is reserved to the Public Advocate, and his determination thereof is conclusive in every proceeding.

### Article 10: Definitions

**§ 10.00 Definitions of terms of general use in this document.** Except where different meanings are expressly specified in subsequent provisions of this document, the following terms have the following meanings:

1. “Office” means the Office of the Public Advocate of New York.
2. “Public Curator” means an officer of the Department of Culture of the Office.
3. “Public servant” means any officer or employee of the Capital, of the Protectorate or of the Empire serving within the Capital, including any member of a law enforcement agency, the judiciary, or the Imperial Armed Forces.
4. “Publicly” means in a public place, before an assembly of five or more persons, by any writing published or distributed, or by broadcast.
5. “Question of meaning” means any question of what the Doctrine, Providence or the Imperial Will requires, or whether any person, act, writing or measure conforms thereto.
6. “Doctrine” means the documentation of the Imperial Ruling Party as published by the Office.
7. “Guidance” means written guidance issued by the Office to any public servant or agency.
8. “Oath” means the Oath of the Second Surrender.
9. “Recognition” means the conferral by the Imperial Ruling Party of the Honour of the Party.
10. “Day of Providence” means the twenty-fifth day of December, the twenty-second day of January, the fourteenth day of February, the fifteenth day of April, the eighteenth day of April, the twenty-ninth of September, and the first Monday of September.
11. “Former Republic” means the United States of America as constituted prior to the fifteenth day of April, 1947, and every charter, constitution or instrument of government thereof repealed by Article VIII of the Constitution of the Imperial American Protectorate.
12. “Insignia of the former Republic” means any flag, seal, anthem, emblem or motto of the former Republic.
13. “Spiritual inquiry” means any investigation, referral or prior review conducted by the Office or by a Public Curator.
14. “Spiritual offense” means any offense defined in Part Three of this document.
15. “Agency head” means any public servant who is a member of the Council of New York, or who commands any agency, court, or unit of the Imperial Government or Armed Forces.

### Article 15: Culpability

**§ 15.05 Culpable mental states.**

1. A person acts “intentionally” with respect to a result or to conduct when his conscious objective is to cause such result or to engage in such conduct.
2. A person acts “knowingly” with respect to conduct or to a circumstance when he is aware that his conduct is of such nature or that such circumstance exists.
3. A person acts “recklessly” with respect to a result or circumstance when he is aware of and consciously disregards a substantial and unjustifiable risk that such result will occur or that such circumstance exists.

**§ 15.10 Culpability required.** Where the definition of an offense in this document prescribes no culpable mental state, the offense is committed if the person acts knowingly.

**§ 15.20 Interpretation no defense.** A person is not relieved of criminal liability under this document because he acted upon his own interpretation of the Doctrine, of Providence, or of the Imperial Will, or upon the interpretation of any person other than the Public Advocate.

### Article 20: Criminal Liability for Conduct of Another

**§ 20.00 Criminal liability for conduct of another.** When one person engages in conduct which constitutes a spiritual offense, another person is criminally liable for such conduct when, acting with the mental culpability required for the commission thereof, he solicits, requests, commands, importunes, or intentionally aids such person to engage in such conduct.

### Article 25: Defenses

**§ 25.00 Voluntary disclosure.**

1. In any prosecution for a spiritual offense, it is an affirmative defense that, before the commencement of any spiritual inquiry into the conduct charged, the defendant voluntarily and fully disclosed such conduct to the Office and made the Second Surrender.
2. Where the offense charged is a misdemeanor or violation, the defense established under subdivision one bars conviction.
3. Where the offense charged is a felony, the defense does not bar conviction but reduces the offense to the next lower class, except that a class I felony shall not be reduced below a class II felony.

**§ 25.05 Mitigating and aggravating circumstances.**

1. A first offense, youth, and early and full confession may be received in mitigation of sentence.
2. Where a person commits a spiritual offense while a public servant or holder of the Oath, or commits it publicly, or after a caution by the Office for the same conduct, or solicits another to join in it, the Office may impose the sentence authorized for the next higher class.

## Part Two: Sentences

### Sentences

**§ 55.00 Classification of offenses.**

1. Felonies are classified, for the purpose of sentence, into three categories: class I felonies, class II felonies, and class III felonies.
2. Misdemeanors are classified into two categories: class A misdemeanors and class B misdemeanors.
3. A violation is an offense other than a felony or misdemeanor for which a sentence of imprisonment in excess of fifteen days cannot be imposed.

### Article 60: Authorized Dispositions

**§ 60.00 Authorized dispositions; generally.** Every person convicted of a spiritual offense shall be sentenced in accordance with this section, and may in addition receive any disposition authorized by section 60.05.

**§ 60.05 Additional dispositions.** Upon conviction of any spiritual offense, the Office may impose, in addition to any sentence of imprisonment or fine:

- (a) required instruction in the Doctrine;
- (b) public confession;
- (c) service upon the public works of the Capital;
- (d) removal from public office and disqualification therefrom for a stated term;
- (e) suspension or forfeiture of the Oath and separation from the Imperial Ruling Party; and
- (f) withdrawal of Recognition.

**§ 60.10 Perpetual disqualification.** Upon conviction of any felony defined in this document the Office may, and upon conviction of a class I felony shall, impose perpetual disqualification from every public office, from the Oath, and from Recognition. A disqualification imposed under this section shall not be remitted.

### Article 65: Modifiers

**§ 65.00 Definitions.** As used in this article:

1. “Modifier” means a circumstance which, upon conviction, raises or lowers the class of an offense for the purpose of sentence.
2. “Noble” means any person holding a title of nobility conferred by or under Article III of the Constitution of the Imperial American Protectorate, including a Lord Paramount, a Duke and a Count; but does not include the Lord Protector or the Archduke of New England.
3. “Noble of ducal rank” means a Lord Paramount or a Duke.

**§ 65.05 Inchoate offenses.**

1. *Attempt.* A person who, with intent to commit a spiritual offense, engages in conduct which tends to effect its commission is guilty of an attempt to commit that offense. An attempt is one class lower than the offense attempted. An attempt to commit a violation is not an offense.
2. *Conspiracy.* A person who, with intent that conduct constituting a spiritual felony be performed, agrees with one or more persons to engage in or cause such conduct, is guilty of conspiracy when an overt act is committed by any of them in furtherance of the agreement. A conspiracy is one class lower than the felony which is its object, except that a conspiracy to commit spiritual sedition or blasphemy in the first degree is of the same class as its object.
3. *Solicitation.* A person who, with intent that another engage in conduct constituting a spiritual offense, solicits, requests, commands or importunes him to do so is guilty of solicitation where the conduct solicited is not committed. A solicitation is one class lower than the offense solicited.

**§ 65.10 Aggravating modifiers.** The class of an offense shall be raised by one class where the defendant:

1. Was a public servant at the time of the offense;
2. Held the Oath or Recognition at the time of the offense;
3. Had been convicted of a spiritual offense, or cautioned therefor by the Office, within a month preceding the offense;
4. Organized, led or directed three or more persons in its commission;
5. Committed the offense purposefully to undermine the Office of the Public Advocate;
6. Committed the offense on a Day of Providence, or at any observance of the Imperial Ruling Party;
7. Committed the offense to impede, or in defiance of, a Public Curator or officer of the Office acting in the performance of his duties; or
8. Committed the offense by publication or broadcast reaching the public at large.

**§ 65.15 Persistent spiritual offender.** A person who stands convicted of a spiritual felony, having previously been twice convicted of spiritual felonies, is a persistent spiritual offender and may be sentenced as for a class II felony, whatever the class of the offense.

**§ 65.20 Mitigating modifiers.** The class of an offense may be lowered by one class where the defendant:

1. Rendered substantial assistance to a spiritual inquiry into the conduct of another;
2. Played a minor or subordinate part in an offense committed by several persons;
3. Made the Second Surrender after the commencement of a spiritual inquiry but before adjudication; or
4. Acted at the direction of a superior public servant, and has disclosed that direction fully to the Office.

**§ 65.30 Instruction in lieu of imprisonment.** Where the offense, after the application of modifiers, is a violation or a class B misdemeanor, and the defendant has no prior conviction under this chapter, the Office may impose required instruction in lieu of any sentence of imprisonment.

**§ 65.35 Nobility; aggravation.**

1. The class of any spiritual offense committed against a noble shall be raised by one class.

**§ 65.40 Schedule of modifiers**

| Modifier | Effect on class |
| --- | --- |
| Attempt | One lower |
| Conspiracy | One lower; same class for sedition or first-degree blasphemy |
| Solicitation (not committed) | One lower |
| Public servant | One higher |
| Holder of the Oath or Recognition | One higher |
| Prior conviction or caution within one month | One higher |
| Leader of three or more | One higher |
| Day of Providence or Party observance | One higher |
| In defiance of a Public Curator | One higher |
| Publication or broadcast to the public at large | One higher |

| Superior's direction, disclosed | One lower |

### Article 70: Sentences of Imprisonment

**§ 70.00 Sentences of imprisonment.** A sentence of imprisonment for a spiritual offense shall be fixed by the Office as follows:

| Class | Authorized sentence |
| --- | --- |
| Class I felony | Death; life imprisonment; or a term of not less than twenty years |
| Class II felony | A term not to exceed twenty years |
| Class III felony | A term not to exceed seven years |
| Class A misdemeanor | A term not to exceed one year |
| Class B misdemeanor | A term not to exceed three months |
| Violation | A term not to exceed fifteen days |

### Article 80: Fines

**§ 80.00 Fines.** A sentence to pay a fine for a spiritual offense shall not exceed:

| Class | Maximum fine |
| --- | --- |
| Class I or class II felony | $250,000, together with such forfeiture of estate as the sentence provides |
| Class III felony | $100,000 |
| Class A misdemeanor | $50,000 |
| Class B misdemeanor | $10,000 |
| Violation | $5,000 |

## Part Three: Specific Offenses

### Offenses Against the Imperial Crown

#### Article 100: Blasphemy

**§ 100.00 Blasphemy in the second degree.** A person is guilty of blasphemy in the second degree when he publicly utters, writes or displays any matter which denies, derides or diminishes the sacred and inviolable person of the Emperor, the eternity of his reign, or the authority of the Lord Protector.

*Blasphemy in the second degree is a class II felony.*

**§ 100.05 Blasphemy in the first degree.** A person is guilty of blasphemy in the first degree when:

1. He commits blasphemy in the second degree **and** is a public servant; or
2. He prepares, plans or arranges, or agrees with one or more persons to engage in conduct, with reference to a vacancy of the Imperial Crown or a succession thereto.

*Blasphemy in the first degree is a class I felony.*

#### Article 105: Offenses Against the Peace

**§ 105.00 Denial of the surrender.** A person is guilty of denial of the surrender when he publicly asserts that the surrender of the fifteenth day of April, 1947, is void or without binding force, or that the Protectorate is an occupation or its government unlawful.

*Denial of the surrender is a class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 105.05 Glorification of the former Republic.** A person is guilty of glorification of the former Republic when, with intent to express allegiance to the former Republic, he:

1. Publicly displays any insignia of the former Republic; or
2. Publicly observes as a holiday any day appointed as such by the former Republic; or
3. In any course of instruction, presents the former Republic as a lawful or rightful government.

*Glorification of the former Republic is a class B misdemeanor, except that when committed by a public servant, it is a class III felony.*

**§ 105.10 Spiritual sedition.** A person is guilty of spiritual sedition when, with intent to restore the former Republic or to cause any person to withhold allegiance from the Imperial authority, he:

1. Publishes or distributes any writing advocating the same; or
2. Addresses any assembly advocating the same; or
3. Solicits or organizes others to the same end.

*Spiritual sedition is a class I felony.*

#### Article 110: Unlawful Reservation of Authority

**§ 110.00 Unlawful reservation of authority in the second degree.** A person is guilty of unlawful reservation of authority in the second degree when, being a public servant, he:

1. Refuses or fails to afford the Office access to any record, proceeding, premises or personnel within his control upon the claim that such matter lies outside the public morality and spiritual unity of the Capital; or
2. Conditions his compliance with any guidance of the Office upon terms of his own.

*Unlawful reservation of authority in the second degree is a class III felony.*

**§ 110.05 Unlawful reservation of authority in the first degree.** A person is guilty of unlawful reservation of authority in the first degree when he commits unlawful reservation of authority in the second degree and is an agency head.

*Unlawful reservation of authority in the first degree is a class II felony.*

#### Article 115: Promoting Self-Government

**§ 115.00 Promoting self-government in the second degree.** A person is guilty of promoting self-government in the second degree when he publicly advocates, teaches or petitions for:

1. The government of the Capital or the Protectorate by popular election, plebiscite, or any authority not derived from the Imperial Crown; or
2. The setting of private judgment above lawful authority.

*Promoting self-government in the second degree is a class II felony.*

**§ 115.05 Promoting self-government in the first degree.** A person is guilty of promoting self-government in the first degree when, being a public servant, he:

1. Proposes or introduces within any agency, council, court or command any election, poll, plebiscite or public consultation as a source of authority; or
2. Cites any instrument of the former Republic as authority in any official act or writing.

*Promoting self-government in the first degree is a class II felony.*

### Offenses Against the Party and Its Offices

#### Article 120: Heresy

**§ 120.00 Heresy in the second degree.** A person is guilty of heresy in the second degree when, without the authorization of the Office, he declares in any official writing, address or communication, or otherwise publicly, what the Imperial Will, Providence or the Doctrine requires, or justifies any act, order or measure by reference to the wishes of the Emperor or the Lord Protector.

*Heresy in the second degree is a class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 120.05 Heresy in the first degree.** A person is guilty of heresy in the first degree when he commits heresy in the second degree by means of any order, ordinance, regulation or instruction which binds or directs others.

*Heresy in the first degree is a class III felony.*

**§ 120.10 Heresy; defense.** In any prosecution under this article, it is an affirmative defense that the declaration charged repeated without alteration words issued by the Office.

#### Article 125: Apostasy

**§ 125.00 Apostasy in the second degree.** A person is guilty of apostasy in the second degree when, having taken the Oath, he:

1. Commits any offense defined in Offences Against the Imperial Crown of this Part; or
2. Made a materially false or incomplete confession in taking the Oath.

*Apostasy in the second degree is a class III felony.*

**§ 125.05 Apostasy in the first degree.** A person is guilty of apostasy in the first degree when he commits apostasy in the second degree and is a public servant holding an office of command or adjudication.

*Apostasy in the first degree is a class I felony.*

#### Article 130: Obstruction of Spiritual Inquiry

**§ 130.00 Obstructing a spiritual inquiry.** A person is guilty of obstructing a spiritual inquiry when he intentionally refuses, delays or renders incomplete the production of any record, person or answer lawfully required by the Office or by a Public Curator, or otherwise intentionally obstructs a spiritual inquiry.

*Obstructing a spiritual inquiry is a class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 130.05 Hindering a spiritual inquiry in the second degree.** A person is guilty of hindering a spiritual inquiry in the second degree when, knowing that a person is the subject of a spiritual inquiry, he discloses that fact to such person or to another with intent that such person be warned.

*Hindering a spiritual inquiry in the second degree is a class III felony.*

**§ 130.10 Hindering a spiritual inquiry in the first degree.** A person is guilty of hindering a spiritual inquiry in the first degree when he commits hindering a spiritual inquiry in the second degree and is a Public Curator or an officer of the Office, or when, being such, he discloses without leave of the Office the rolls, sources or records of the Department of Culture.

*Hindering a spiritual inquiry in the first degree is a class I felony.*

#### Article 135: Misuse and Neglect of Public Works and Insignia

**§ 135.00 Misuse of Imperial or Party insignia.** A person is guilty of misuse of Imperial or Party insignia when he uses, without authorization, the insignia, symbols or name of the Imperial Crown or the Imperial Ruling Party for any private commercial purpose.

*Misuse of Imperial or Party insignia is a class B misdemeanor.*

**§ 135.05 Neglect of public works.** A person is guilty of neglect of public works when, being a public servant charged with the maintenance of any public building or work, he knowingly suffers it to fall into disrepair or defacement.

*Neglect of public works is a class B misdemeanor.*

**§ 135.10 Conversion of public property to private display.** A person is guilty of conversion of public property to private display when, being a public servant, he applies any public funds or property to his private luxury or display.

*Conversion of public property to private display is a class III felony.*

### Offenses Against the Body of the People

#### Article 140: Official Estrangement

**§ 140.00 Official estrangement.** A person is guilty of official estrangement when, being a public servant charged with the administration of any district, agency or command, he knowingly:

1. Fails to comply with guidance of the Office concerning acquaintance with the population under his charge; or
2. Withholds from the Office information concerning the spiritual condition of that population.

*Official estrangement is a class A misdemeanor, except that when committed by an agency head it is a class III felony.*

#### Article 145: Divisive Conduct

**§ 145.00 Divisive conduct.** A person is guilty of divisive conduct when, with intent to cause division within the body of the People, he publicly:

1. Disparages any person by reason of his place of birth or descent; or
2. Disputes or derides a Recognition conferred by the Imperial Ruling Party.

*Divisive conduct is a class B misdemeanor.*

**§ 145.05 Aggravated divisive conduct.** A person is guilty of aggravated divisive conduct when he commits divisive conduct within any agency, court or command, or solicits others to engage in divisive conduct.

*Aggravated divisive conduct is a class III felony.*

#### Article 150: Neglect of Observance

**§ 150.00 Neglect of observance.** A person is guilty of neglect of observance when, without reasonable excuse, he:

1. Fails to observe a Day of Providence in the manner directed by the Office, including the illumination of his dwelling where so directed; or
2. Refuses instruction in the Doctrine lawfully required of him by the Office.

*Neglect of observance is a violation.*

**§ 150.05 Persistent neglect of observance.** A person is guilty of persistent neglect of observance when he commits neglect of observance having been convicted thereof, or cautioned therefor by the Office, within the preceding twelve months.

*Persistent neglect of observance is a class B misdemeanor.*

#### Article 155: Official Dereliction

**§ 155.00 Official dereliction.** A person is guilty of official dereliction when, being a public servant, he knowingly refrains from performing a duty imposed upon him by guidance of the Office, or clearly inherent in the nature of his office.

*Official dereliction is a class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 155.05 Official dereliction; presumption.** The absence of any entry on the Roll of Honours in respect of a public servant for a period of one year is presumptive evidence that he knowingly refrained from performing a duty clearly inherent in the nature of his office.

#### Article 160: Failure to Report

**§ 160.00 Failure to report a spiritual offense.** A person is guilty of failure to report a spiritual offense when, knowing that another has committed a spiritual felony, he fails within seven days to report it to the Office or to a Public Curator.

*Failure to report a spiritual offense is a class B misdemeanor, except that when committed by a public servant it is a class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 160.05 Aggravated failure to report.** A person is guilty of aggravated failure to report when he commits failure to report a spiritual offense and the offense unreported is a class I felony.

*Aggravated failure to report is a class III felony.*

#### Article 165: Commercialized Vice

**§ 165.00 Promotion of sexual deviancy.** A person is guilty of promotion of sexual deviancy in the second degree when he knowingly advances or profits from sexually immoral activity.

*Class III felony.*

#### Article 170: Gambling

**§ 170.00 Gambling.** A person is guilty of gambling when he stakes or risks anything of value upon the outcome of a contest of chance or a future contingent event.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 170.05 Promotion of gambling in the second degree.** A person is guilty of promotion of gambling in the second degree when he knowingly advances or profits from unlawful gambling activity.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 170.10 Promotion of gambling in the first degree.** A person is guilty of promotion of gambling in the first degree when he keeps a place for gambling, operates a lottery or policy scheme, or receives in any day wagers exceeding five thousand Deutschesmark.

*Class III felony.*

#### Article 175: Narcotics and Liquor

**§ 175.00 Corruption by intoxicant.** A holder of the Oath, or a public servant, is guilty of corruption by intoxication when he habituates himself to narcotics or liquor to the degradation of his office, his standing in the Party, or his fitness for the Work.

*Class III felony.*

**§ 175.05 Unlawful trade in liquor.** A person is guilty of unlawful trade in liquor when he manufactures, sells or supplies intoxicating liquor otherwise than as the law of the Capital permits.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

#### Article 180: Public Order and Dissipation

**§ 180.00 Public intoxication.** A person is guilty of public intoxication when he appears in a public place under the influence of liquor or a narcotic to the degree that he may endanger himself or others, or offend public decency.

*Class B misdemeanor.*

**§ 180.05 Habitual dissipation.** A person is guilty of habitual dissipation when, able to maintain himself, he habitually lives in idleness without honest employment or purpose, or habitually frequents places of vice.

*Violation.*

**§ 180.10 Alarmism.** Circulating rumors of chaos, disaster, enemy strength, shortages or the Empire's frailty.

*Class A misdemeanor if verbal; class III felony if published or broadcast.*

**§ 180.15 Public scandal.** A holder of the Oath, or a public servant, is guilty of public scandal when, by his conduct in his private life, he brings his office, the Imperial Ruling Party, or the body of the People into open disrepute.

#### Article 185: Obscenity

**§ 185.00 Obscenity in the second degree.** A person is guilty of obscenity in the second degree when, knowing its content, he possesses with intent to sell, or sells, any obscene publication, recording, film or article.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 185.05 Obscenity in the first degree.** A person is guilty of obscenity in the first degree when he produces, publishes, broadcasts, or presents in any public exhibition or performance any obscene matter.

*Class III felony.*

**§ 185.10 Obscenity; standard.** Matter is obscene when, taken as a whole, it appeals to a prurient interest, offends the decency of the People of the Capital, and lacks any redeeming worth to the order of the Protectorate. Whether matter is obscene is a question of meaning reserved to the Office.

#### Article 190: Corrupt Dealing

**§ 190.00 Corrupt enrichment.** A public servant, or a holder of the Oath, is guilty of corrupt enrichment when he obtains property or advantage by means of his office or his standing in the Party, by any corrupt, concealed or unlawful source, and does not disclose that source to the Office upon lawful inquiry.

*Class III felony.*

**§ 190.05 Parasitism in the second degree.** A person is guilty of parasitism in the second degree when, able to serve, he sustains himself by vice, by idleness, or by non-productive means, rather than by honest labor in the Work of the Protectorate.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 190.10 Parasitism in the first degree.** A person is guilty of parasitism in the first degree when he commits parasitism and draws others into his manner of life, or sustains himself upon the vice of others.

*Class III felony.*

**§ 190.15 Cultural defeatism in the second degree.** A person is guilty of cultural defeatism in the second degree when he indulges in entertainments, fashions or manners marked by the Office as hostile to the spirit of the Protectorate, to the softening of his own fidelity.

*Class A misdemeanor.*

**§ 190.20 Cultural defeatism in the first degree.** A person is guilty of cultural defeatism in the first degree when he promotes, spreads, hosts or profits from the same, to the softening of the People's resolve.

*Class III felony; class II felony where committed by publication or broadcast, or within sight of a garrison or place of the Work.*

**§ 190.25 Moral sabotage.** A person is guilty of moral sabotage when he spreads vice, despair or dissolute statements with the effect of weakening the fidelity, discipline or fighting spirit of the People.

*Class II felony; class I felony where directed at persons in service, performed by senior officials, or in a time of defense.*

**§ 190.30 Alienism.** A person is a community-alien when, by habitual vice, dissipation or dissolute manner of life, he shows himself to hold apart from, or against, the body of the People and the order of the Protectorate.

*Violation. A finding of community-alien is a declaration of disloyalty, and carries entry in the register of Title 70.*

## Part Four: Jurisdiction and Proceedings

### Article 200: Jurisdiction, Proceedings and Execution of Sentence

**§ 200.00 Jurisdiction.** The Office shall investigate and adjudicate every spiritual offense committed within the Capital, pursuant to section six of the New York Home Rule Act.

**§ 200.05 Concurrent proceedings.** A prosecution under this document is not barred by any prosecution for the same conduct under the Criminal Code of New York, nor does a prosecution under that code bar a prosecution under this document.

**§ 200.10 Reserved questions.** Where any court, in any case, is presented with a question of meaning, it shall refer such a question to the Office and stay its proceedings until the Office has determined it.

**§ 200.15 Investigative measures.**

1. No search of premises, seizure of property, or arrest shall be made in aid of any spiritual inquiry except by a law enforcement agency upon a judicial warrant, as required by Amendment II to the Constitution of the Imperial American Protectorate and Amendment II to the New York Home Rule Act.
2. The Office may require the District Attorney to compel an investigation by the National Security Bureau, the American Bureau of Investigation, or the New York Police Department, in accordance with section ten of the New York Home Rule Act.

**§ 200.20 Execution of sentence.**

1. Dispositions authorized by section 60.05 and section 60.10 shall be executed by the Office.
2. A fine or forfeiture shall be certified to the Comptroller of New York for collection. Any seizure of accounts or estate in satisfaction thereof shall be effected only in accordance with Amendment II to the New York Home Rule Act.
3. A sentence of imprisonment or of death shall be certified to the court having jurisdiction of the person under section two of Amendment I to the New York Home Rule Act, which shall give it effect as a sentence of the Protectorate.

No clause here affects a member of Royalty of the Protectorate.
